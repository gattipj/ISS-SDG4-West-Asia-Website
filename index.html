Introduction - What are the "SDGs"?

The 17 Sustainable Development goals(SDGs) were unanimously adopted by all 193 member states of the United Nations in September of 2015. Their purpose was to illustrate the 17 most important criterion the countries of the world must have in order to achieve global sustainability by 2030. A sustainable world according to the UN is a world where humanity is replenishing the resources they are consuming and future generations won't have to worry about resource shortages or global conflict. Since its adoption, progress and awareness of the SDGs have ebbed and flowed. The goals quickly entered the world of politics as people began debating the necessity and importance of the SDGs, especially when compared to more popular political issues. Sometimes certain goals will come to the forefront of peoples minds as the consequences of inaction become more prevalent and necessary to address. While others will shy away from the SDGs when more pressing issues arise or when supporting the goals could hinder their own personal agenda. The purpose of this website is to highlight the importance of one SDG, SDG 4, and to bring awareness to a lesser known country who has made significant progress toward achieving this goal in recent years.

What is SDG 4?

Sustainable Development Goal 4 is "Quality Education" and broadly speaking, is to "Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all" (United Nations Website, 2015). It encompasses everything that has to do with education. From the number of students admitted to school to the number of qualified teachers working at said schools. Some examples of metrics that define if a country is "on track" to reach the goal are high primary school admission rate, high percentage of young students who are literate, high percentage of students who complete primary school, etc. Similar to other SDGs, SDG 4 has multiple "targets" and "indicators" that separate the goal into specific sections. These targets highlight specific points of interest that the UN decided was necessary to have "Quality Education" in a country. While indicators determine how progress can be tracked with each indicator having their own threshold to reach. A country must be working on most if not all of the targets in order for them to be considered on track to reach a goal. This website will focus on three targets of SDG 4, equal access to pre-primary education(4.2), eliminate all discrimination in education(4.5), and universal literacy and numeracy(4.6).

Qatar

Qatar, is a country located in the Middle East, specifically in the Persian Gulf(Arabian Gulf) and shares a border with Saudi Arabia. In the last decade, the country has made incredible progress towards SDG 4. They have increased their spending on education to approximately 7 billion US dollars per year, and ranked first globally in gender parity in primary and tertiary education, according to a 2024 report by the World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Report in 2024. This came as the country made a National Statement of Commitment where they vowed to transform their education system and highlight education as a critical role in the future of the country.

Target 4.2: Equal Access to Quality Pre-primary Education

This graph depicts the percentage of children that were enrolled in pre-primary education since the turn of the century. Significant progress has been made as the country began to focus on providing children with quality education while simultaneously vowing to eliminate gender discrimination in education. However, a gulf dispute in 2017 led international students to become displaced and unable to continue their education. Furthermore, in 2019 the COVID-19 pandemic negatively impacted the percentage of participation due to economic and safety concerns. However, Qatar still made significant progress overall because they increased the total percentage by nearly 23% in such a short time, and they are considered on track to reach the goal of 100% participation of children in pre-primary education by 2030.

Target 4.5: Eliminate All Discrimination in Education

The graph above demonstrates how equal male and female students are in achieving the minimum proficiency in reading and math (The minimum proficiency level required was decided by the UN). The gap between male and female proficiency has closed by a decent margin in recent years, meaning that gender discrimination has decreased as men and women preform at more similar levels. Interestingly, there is an outlier where girls in lower secondary school preform significantly better than boys in reading.

This graph shows the parity index or how equal children from high and low income families reach a minimum proficiency level in reading and math. Unfortunately, this graph represents an indicator that Qatar has not made much progress in achieving. Wealthier individuals are always going to be able to give their child more resources to learn. Furthermore, secondary school requires more funding and resources to help a child learn due to its increased difficulty compared to primary school. Despite the large sums of money Qatar has put towards public education. They do not provide enough to each individual low income child to decrease the gap.

Target 4.6: Universal Literacy and Numeracy

This graph shows the percentage of people who are literate filtered by age group and gender. This graph is interesting because the improvement isn't quite as obvious. At first glance the literacy rate of young adults looks stagnant, however, Qatars unwavering dedication to eradicating illiteracy has inched the literacy rate closer to 100%. Furthermore, you can see the progress reflected in the older generations.

Closing Statements

It is clear that Qatar has made huge progress in SDG 4. Their overhaul of the education system and making education a focal point for the country to focus on worked as enrollment increased and gender discrimination decreased. While they still have much to work towards especially grappling with economic differences in education, they have made monumental progress and stands as a role model for the improvement of children's education in a country.
