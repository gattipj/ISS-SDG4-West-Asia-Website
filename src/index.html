Introduction

The 17 Sustainable Development goals(SDGs) were unanimously adopted by all 193 member states of the United Nations in September of 2015. Since then, progress and awareness of the SDGs have ebbed and flowed. World events have shifted focus to and away from the SDGs, while the goals themselves became politicized as people from all different backgrounds began debating the necessity and importance of certain SDGs. This website aims to describe the importance of SDG 4, quality education, and to showcase the progress made by some countries who do not typically make the news on the global stage.

What is SDG 4?

Sustainable Development Goal 4 is to "Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all". It encompasses everything that has to do with education. From the percentage of admittance to the number of qualified teachers working at any given time. Some metrics that define if a country is on track to reach the goal are high primary school admission rate, high percentage of young students who are literate, high percentage of students who complete primary school, etc.

Qatar

Qatar, is a country located in West Asia in the Persian Gulf(Arabian Gulf) and shares a border with Saudi Arabia. In the last decade, the country has made incredible progress towards SDG 4. They have increased their spending on education to approximately 7 billion US dollars per year, and ranked first globally in gender parity in primary and tertiary education, according to a 2024 report by the World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Report in 2024.

This graph depicts the percentage of children that were enrolled in pre-primary education since the turn of the century. Significant progress has been made as the country began to focus on providing children with quality education while simultaneously vowing to eliminate gender discrimination in education. A gulf dispute in 2017 and the COVID-19 pandemic in 2019, negatively impacted the percentage of participation due to economic and safety concerns. However, Qatar still made significant progress overall because they increased the total percentage by nearly 23% in such a short time, and they are considered on track to reach the goal of 100% participation of children in pre-primary education by 2030.

This graph shows the percentage of people who are literate filtered by age group and gender. This graph is interesting because the improvement isn't quite as obvious. At first glance the literacy rate of young adults looks stagnant, however, Qatars unwavering dedication to eradicating illiteracy has inched the literacy rate closer to 100%. Furthermore, you can see the progress reflected in the older generations.
